A Few Unforgettable
Weeks at JHU

A personal run-down of my summer math experience at Johns Hopkins University — what I learned, who I met, and the strange-satisfying corners of infinity that made me grin.

Johns Hopkins University
Math Summer Program
Academic

Getting Started

Why I chose this program

I wanted to be around people who love math as much as I do — and to dive deeper into strange, interesting concepts far beyond what school offers.

First impressions

Campus felt familiar yet electric. The kind of place where late-night problem-solving is completely natural. What surprised me most: how normal it felt to talk about math over dinner.

Meeting my people

Conversations quickly turned into problem swaps and curiosity spirals. That immediate sense of shared nerdiness is one of the best parts of the whole experience — you stop explaining why you care and just start talking.

The Math Itself

Aha! Moment — Infinitely Many Primes

I had a real "math detective" moment when I worked through the proof that there are infinitely many primes of the form 4k + 3. That kind of argument — simultaneously clever and inevitable — made me feel like I was unlocking a quiet secret about numbers that had always been there, waiting.

Problem milestone

I was particularly proud of constructing a proof related to Fermat's right-triangle theorem. A great payoff after wrestling with the idea.

New vocabulary

The program gave me the words and formal concepts to express what I was already curious about — which made both thinking and communicating math far cleaner.

Math Spotlight

⋆ Featured Concept

The Banach–Tarski Paradox

A theorem in set-theoretic geometry stating that a solid ball in 3D space can be decomposed into a finite number of non-overlapping pieces, then reassembled — using only rotations and translations — into two identical copies of the original ball.

Why it matters

It's funny, weird, and somehow delightful. It highlights how infinity behaves in ways that utterly clash with physical intuition. I found myself tracing connections to possible ideas in quantum mechanics — how the unmeasured can be stranger than the measured.

"You know you're at a math camp when casual conversation is about math problems — and both people are genuinely interested."

Beyond the Numbers

A typical day had a clear, efficient rhythm: lessons → break → lessons → food → optional free time. Focused and distraction-free — perfect for diving deep. There weren't many extra events or field trips, but the steady routine let me fully concentrate. Sometimes the best structure is the absence of noise.

The People

Most students were from the US, so there wasn't a huge international mix — but the people I met were engaged and genuinely curious. While no single person defined the experience, the overall vibe of students and mentors who took math seriously left a quiet, lasting impression. The collective energy is its own thing.

Growth & Reflection

How my thinking changed

The program broadened my mathematical vocabulary and opened doors to new fields. It gave me words for curiosities I already had — and that made everything sharper.

Advice to past-me

Relax and embrace the small randomness. The best parts aren't always the big theorems — they're the tiny discoveries and side conversations that happen between classes.

Fun & Personal

One charming constant: casual conversation inevitably turned into a math problem — and both sides were completely into it. Strange moments, inside jokes about infinity, and an oddly comforting sense that weirdness was the norm.

I'll miss the fun randomness most of all — the small, unplanned moments that somehow outweigh every planned lecture.

The Big Picture

Did the experience change my long-term goals? Not significantly — but it reaffirmed my love for exploring new mathematical territory. Would I recommend the program? Yes. And my only tip: go in a little blind. Let the program surprise you rather than overplanning what you expect to get out of it.
